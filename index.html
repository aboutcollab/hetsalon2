Het Salon 2 · Centrale Bakkerij · Amsterdam Sloterdijk · opens December 2026

We're Studio About — an event and workshop space, with six studios for makers.

We host art workshops and creative events in a 20 × 12 m hall inside the Het Salon art community, next to Sloterdijk station. Around our floor: six studios to rent — two on the ground floor, four up on the deck — for makers and creators in the same line as us. A small community inside a big one, with a shared saw room, one all-in price — and rent that falls when our events do well.

The part that is genuinely unusual

When we win, you win.

We run art workshops all over the Netherlands, and part of why we are building this space is so we can host them here too. Here is the deal we want with the people who rent from us: when a workshop of ours runs, your rent that month goes down. Two situations, and this is exactly what each one asks of you.

Small workshops — under 40 people
2% off

We close our curtains and stay on our own floor. All we ask is that you don't make noise that would carry into the room — you stay in your plot, you keep working, you just don't run anything loud for four or five hours. Most of what we do is this kind, and every single time we ask, 2% comes off your rent.

Bigger events — the common area too
4% off

Sometimes an event needs the shared floor as well, so for the same four or five hours we need the common area clear. Your own plot is still yours. We expect one or two of these a month, and each one takes 4% off your rent.

When this actually happens — and why it barely touches you

In general our workshops run inside office hours on weekdays — in practice that is typically a Tuesday or Thursday afternoon, four or five hours at a time — and you always get the schedule well in advance. As we grow, the timing can vary; the deal doesn't: every workshop we run takes money off your rent that month, and the credits stack, month after month, with no ceiling — the better our workshops do, the less you pay.

Before you ask

The questions everybody has.

Can I use the wood machines?

What comes with Studios 1 and 2 is the 36 m² wood and saw room itself — an enclosed space designed for that work, shared between those two plots and About Collab. We are kitting it out between us: we bring machines and we expect you'll bring some of yours. If you take a plot on the deck, the saw room isn't part of it — you still get build-up room on the event floor like everyone. That is the difference between the two prices, and most of the €250 between them.

Can I use the big open floor? Can I leave things there?

Use it, yes — that floor is our event and workshop space first, and when nothing is on, any plot can use it for a build-up: the piece or the job that doesn't fit your own studio, under the full 8 m. Leave things there, no — it is a build space, not storage. Your material and your work live in your own plot, so the floor is clear when the next workshop loads in. That rule is what keeps a shared floor usable for everybody, including you.

What does "you help build the space" mean?

The hall is being built out this autumn — the deck, the saw room, the fit-out. The two ground plots go to makers who want a hand in that: people who build things for a living, building the place they'll then work in. It is part of the deal on that floor — how much and when is agreed person by person, before anything is signed. It is also, honestly, the best way there is to know exactly what you're renting.

Who are you looking for?

For the two ground plots: woodworkers and furniture makers who are on machines most days — and who want a hand in building the space, because that is part of the deal on that floor. For the deck: makers, small studios, small businesses and collectives who want their own base, room to grow into when a job is big, and somewhere to teach or host now and then. The building's own plan calls this zone zelfbouw ateliers voor houtbewerkers en installatiebouwers — self-build studios for woodworkers and installers. That is exactly who it is for.

What's the light like in there?

It is an old freezer hall, so the light inside is the light we build — and we're building a lot of it, with plants. The daylight is the garden: big, right outside, always open to you, and good to work in, eat in or take a call in when the weather is decent.

Can I share a plot?

Yes, and most people will. One contract with us, one price, and you settle the rest between you — we don't price per person and we don't need to know the arrangement.

What does it cost to use the event space beyond my free hours?

€10 an hour, which is roughly a third of what a neighbour elsewhere in Het Salon pays and a fraction of the outside rate. On top of that there is a cleaning share per booking: €25 for a workshop or a regular event, up to 20 people · €40 for up to 40 people · €50 for a full party.

How do I get there, and can I park?

The Centrale Bakkerij, a few minutes' walk from Amsterdam Sloterdijk — intercity, metro, tram and bus. Parking on site, and a loading route for anyone who moves material.

Who is the landlord?

Studio About and About Collab — construction, creative production and art workshops — working out of the same hall. Your landlord is down the corridor with sawdust on their shoes, not in an office park.

When can I move in?

The space opens in December 2026. Talk to us now and you choose your plot.

If you want one

How this actually goes.

We are in the middle of building this: signing our lease with the building, arranging the funding, planning the build — and finding our tenants. Showing interest now costs you nothing and shapes the plan with us. And we'll be honest the other way too: we choose our tenants as carefully as you choose your space — this only works if we match.

  1. Write to us. Which plot interests you, what you make or do, and whether you'd take it alone or with somebody.
  2. We have a chat. What you need, what the space offers, whether the community fits — both ways. If we match, we keep going.
  3. We plan it together. Your name against a specific plot at a specific price, and the month you'd want to start. No money, no signature, no obligation either way.
  4. We sign our lease, then you sign yours. The building's contract first, then your contract with us — and your rent starts when you can get in the door and put your things down.
